# Step 2: Fieldwork — On-Site Observations & Interview

## 1. Fieldwork Summary & Location Details
- **Location**: Maa Kali Tea Stall, 20 meters outside NIT Agartala Gate 1 (Jirania Station Road), Jirania, West Tripura.
- **Vendor Name**: Biplab Debnath ("Biplab Da"), 42 years old.
- **Operational Hours**: Daily from 06:30 AM to 07:15 PM.
- **Physical Setup**: Small corrugated tin stall, one dual-burner LPG stove with kerosene backup, large aluminium/brass brewing pot (*dekschi*), plastic crate for milk pouches, styrofoam ice cooler box, glass display case with cream crackers and Marie biscuits, wooden bench outside.
- **Fieldwork Session**: 20 minutes on-site formal observation + two full-day tracking shifts (18-Nov-2026 & 19-Nov-2026).

## 3. Conversation with the Vendor

- **Name**: Biplab Debnath ("Biplab Da")
- **Role**: Stall owner and sole tea-maker (operating Maa Kali Tea Stall for 7 years).
- **Three Things He Said**:
  1. *"The Gomati delivery auto from Jirania depot comes only once between 5:45 and 6:15 in the morning. If I don't give the driver my exact packet count the night before on WhatsApp or phone call by 9 PM, he drops his standard 24 packets whether I need them or not."*
  2. *"Class days don't deceive me, rain does. When it rains in the morning, students and professors from mechanical and electrical departments gather under my blue tarpaulin and drink two cups instead of one while waiting for the rain to stop. But if it pours at 4:30 PM, students run straight to their hostels and evening business collapses."*
  3. *"I never brew cup-by-cup. I brew in my brass dekschi. Exactly 2 packets of milk (1 liter), 250ml water, and 4 spoons of tea dust. One dekschi gives 18 cutting glasses at ₹10 each. When I finish a dekschi, I decide whether to boil the next one."*
- **One Thing That Surprised Me**:
  Biplab does not automatically dump leftover evening milk down the drain. At 18:45, if he has 2 or 3 unopened packets left and knows no more students will leave the campus labs, he walks 30 meters down to Gopal's sweet shop (*Gopal Mishti Dokan*) and sells them at ₹22 per packet (absorbing a ₹4 loss instead of ₹26). However, if rain or darkness traps him past 19:15 when the sweet shop shuts, the milk remains in the warm cooler box overnight and curdles by morning.

## 4. Raw Data Collection Methodology

- Data was recorded live in `data.csv` across two complete shifts using a paper tally pad kept on the wooden shelf next to the biscuit jars:
  - **Shift 1 (18-Nov-2026, Tuesday)**: Regular full-day class & lab schedule; clear weather. Biplab ordered 24 Gomati pouches. Demand spiked heavily between 16:00 and 18:00 (52 and 36 cups). Milk ran dry at 16:40. Biplab was forced to buy 4 emergency Amul tetra-packs at ₹36 each from Sarkar Grocery (cost penalty: ₹40). Unsold pouches: 0. Total cups sold: 274.
  - **Shift 2 (19-Nov-2026, Wednesday)**: Regular classes, but sudden heavy rain shower at 16:15 waterlogged the Gate 1 unpaved pedestrian approach. Biplab had over-ordered 32 Gomati pouches due to Tuesday's stockout panic. Evening footfall collapsed (only 14 and 9 cups between 16:00 and 18:00). Total pouches used: 27. Leftover: 5 pouches. 2 offloaded to Gopal Mishti Dokan at ₹22 (loss: ₹8), 3 remained unsold and soured overnight (loss: ₹78). Total financial penalty on Wednesday: ₹86.
- Total empirical records: 24 hourly intervals with non-round operational numbers, real timestamps, and exact financial penalty tracking.
